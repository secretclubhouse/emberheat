# Carrier board enclosure: design

Date: 2026-11-15. Companion to `2026-11-15-carrier-board-design.md`. Status: awaiting the owner's approval.

## Intent

A 3D-printed box for the rev 1 carrier board (`hardware/carrier-board/`), so the board hangs on the wall on a 35 mm DIN rail with nothing live or fragile exposed, and can be opened by hand.

Why: the bare board on DIN clips leaves solder joints facing a metal rail at an unknown distance, and leaves the dev boards and relays open to knocks and dust. A box puts plastic between the board and the rail and covers the front.

## Goals

- The board screws to four printed standoffs through its M3 corner holes, into heat-set inserts.
- The box, not the board, carries the two DIN clips (Hammond 1427DINCLIP, already on the BOM). Wall-mounted, zone terminals at the top.
- The cover lifts off by hand: magnets, no screws.
- With the cover on: the seven zone plugs and the power input (USB-C and the 5 V screw terminal) stay plugged in and reachable; the seven zone lights are visible through a window.
- With the cover off: the board is open on all four sides for wiring and meter checks.
- Prints on a 300 mm bed without supports.
- The model is generated by a script that takes the board's dimensions from the board's own files, so the two cannot drift.

## Non-goals

- Sealing against dust or water. No gaskets.
- Reaching the dev boards' USB ports or buttons with the cover on. Flashing means lifting the cover.
- Light pipes. The window is a plain opening with an optional clear pane.
- Strain relief or cable glands for the zone wires.
- Any change to the board. Its own DIN clip holes (H5, H6) go unused.

## Anti-goals

- Any magnet or other metal added near the two antennas.
- A cover that can slide down the wall or fall off when a zone plug is pulled.
- A cover that cannot lift straight off because part of it sits under the board or behind a plug.
- Dimensions typed into the enclosure script that duplicate the board's.

## Constraints from the board

Board coordinates: x from the left edge, y down from the top edge, in mm. The board is 168 x 114 x 1.6 mm with a notch 67.24 x 33.40 mm in the bottom edge. Figures marked ~ are to be read from the board file by the script, not copied from here.

- Corner holes, M3: (4, 4), (164, 4), (4, 110), (164, 110).
- Round each corner hole the back of the board is free of pads only to 4.0 mm from the hole's centre (4.44 mm at the two bottom holes). So where a standoff touches the board, it may reach at most 3.5 mm from the hole's centre on the sides that face the board. It may be thicker lower down and toward the corner.
- Antennas: the C6 module overhangs the left edge by 6.0 mm and the H2 module the right edge by 5.05 mm, both in the bottom band (y ~80 to ~106), about 10 mm above the board.
- Zone terminals: seven right-angle headers on the top edge, plug mouths at the edge, centres at x ~40.2 + 18 (n - 1). Plug body 10.2 mm wide; plugs stick out past the top edge. Wires leave upward.
- Power, left edge: USB-C socket J1 at y ~42, mouth 0.6 mm past the edge; screw terminal J2 at y ~55, wires entering from the left.
- Zone lights: seven 3 mm LEDs at x ~35.1 + 18 (n - 1), y ~40. Power light D8 at ~(2.2, 72.3).
- The positions above for the lights are footprint origins (pad 1), not lens centres. The script works out each lens centre from the footprint in the board file.
- Heights above the board's top face were never checked against parts. Use these for the clearance model: relay 15 mm; varistor 12 mm; fuse 14 mm; C1 11.5 mm; J2 10 mm; terminal header and plug 15 mm; dev boards: underside 11 mm, top of everything on them 17 mm (so 17 mm is the tallest); antenna boxes from 11 to 15 mm.
- Zone plug (Phoenix 1757019): model it as a box 10.2 mm wide and 15 mm tall that runs from the header mouth to 15 mm past the board's top edge, so it passes right through the wall. The wires bend outside the box.
- DIN clip: body 45.4 x 10 x 14 mm, two screw holes 29.75 +/- 0.75 mm apart, rail centre 16.14 mm from the latch-end hole toward the hook. Fits hook end up only. Supplied screws are #6 x 1/4 in pan head, self-tapping into the clip, and need about 1.6 mm of material under the head (as on the board).

## Approaches

1. **Shallow base, deep cover (chosen by the owner).** The base is a plate with a low locating lip, the standoffs and the clips. The cover is a five-sided shell. With the cover off, the board is open on all sides.
2. Deep tray, flat lid. Stiffer and the lid is a plain plate, but the board sits in a well, which makes probing and plugging awkward. Rejected for that.
3. Clips on the board, cover only. Rejected: it keeps the unknown gap between solder joints and the rail.

## Design

Sizes below are the starting values; every one is a named parameter in the script.

**Envelope.** Inside 186 x 117 mm: 9 mm beside the board on the left and right (antenna overhang, and room for the magnet pads), 1 mm above the top edge, 2 mm below the bottom edge. Walls 2.4 mm, floor 3 mm, cover face 2 mm. Outside about 191 x 122 x 38 mm, plus 14 mm of clip behind.

**Base.**
- Floor plate the full outside size. The cover's walls sit on its edge.
- A locating lip 4 mm high just inside the cover walls, 0.25 mm clear of them, broken where the cover has openings or magnet pads. It stops the cover sliding in any direction along the wall.
- Four standoffs under the corner holes, board back 6 mm above the floor. Each takes an M3 heat-set insert from the top. Default insert: M3, 5 mm outside diameter, 4 mm long (the Voron standard); hole size is a parameter.
- Two DIN clips on the back, hook end up, rail centre line across the middle of the box, clips about 40 mm in from each side. Each clip has one round 4.0 mm hole (latch end) and one slot 4.0 x 5.5 mm (hook end), as on the board. The screws go in from inside the box, each in a counterbore that leaves 1.6 mm of floor under the head, so the supplied screws reach.
- The back is marked `HOOK` and `UP` by each clip, debossed.

**Cover.**
- Clear height above the board: 25 mm (tallest modelled part 17 mm; the margin is for heights nobody has measured). The cover's inside face is then about 20 mm above the zone lights.
- Top wall: seven notches, one per zone plug, open at the cover's edge so the cover lifts off over the plugs. Each 13 mm wide and reaching 17 mm above the board's top face.
- Left wall: one notch for J1 and J2, open at the cover's edge like the top notches so the cover lifts off with the cables in place, y ~34 to ~64, reaching 12 mm above the board's top face. Wide enough for a USB-C plug's moulded body, which has to enter the wall because the socket is 9 mm inside it.
- Bottom wall: a row of vent slots. The terminal notches are the outlet at the top.
- Window: one slot over the seven zone lights, about 122 x 12 mm, cut through the cover and through the dial strip in front of it. Two rails on the cover's inside face hold a clear pane (about 126 x 16 x 1.5 mm, printed separately) that slides in from one end against a stop at the other. The box works without the pane.
- A 3 mm hole over the power light D8.

**Dial strip.** A separate plate, 2 mm thick, that sits in a recess in the cover's face so its surface is flush with the face. It is glued or pressed in.
- It carries the window slot, the numerals 1 to 7 and the wordmark `emberheat` (lowercase).
- The lettering stands 0.4 mm proud of the strip. The strip prints flat, lettering up, in the body colour, with one filament change to black for the last 0.4 mm. The README gives the height of the change.
- Each numeral is centred under its light, below the window, about 3 mm tall. The wordmark is about 3 mm tall, in a lower corner of the strip, sharing a margin or baseline with the numerals.
- Typeface: Inter (open licence), the font file committed beside the script so every build gives the same letters.
- The strip can be reprinted alone to change the labels.

**Styling.** The owner wants things made for the house to follow Braun's product design of the 1950s to 1970s. Here that means:
- flat faces; the four vertical edges rounded at about 8 mm outside; a small even chamfer on the other outside edges; nothing ornamental;
- body in off-white or light grey, lettering black, no other colour (the lights are the accent);
- every marking and opening on a common grid: numerals under lights, strip margins equal, vents evenly spaced and centred;
- the vents as a tidy field of round holes or even slots, like a speaker grille.

The rounded corners must not cut into the 9 mm side gaps where the magnet pads sit, or the pads move to suit.

**Magnets.** Four pairs of 6 x 3 mm discs (parameter), meeting at the line where the cover sits on the base, pressed and glued into pockets that face each other. Positions: one pair beside each top corner of the board, in the 9 mm side gaps, and two against the bottom wall inside the board's notch. None within 20 mm of either antenna. No cover feature sits under the board.

**Fits.** 0.25 mm between sliding or mating printed faces; holes for screws 0.4 mm over the thread.

**Printing.** Base: floor on the bed. Cover: face on the bed, so the face takes the bed's texture; the strip's recess is a short bridge hidden behind the strip. Dial strip: flat, lettering up, one colour change. Pane: flat. No supports; overhangs under the window rails are chamfered at 45 degrees. Material is the owner's choice (ABS, ASA or PETG all suit).

## The script and its checks

- Location: `hardware/carrier-board/enclosure/`. A Python script using CadQuery writes `base`, `cover`, `dial-strip` and `pane` as STEP and STL, and renders images of the box open and closed for the owner to look at.
- CadQuery lives in a virtual environment in the git-ignored `hardware/carrier-board/tools/`. A `make enclosure` target builds everything; a `make` target or documented step creates the environment.
- Board positions (outline, notch, corner holes, terminal, J1, J2, light and D8 positions, antenna overhangs) come from the board's files. A check fails if the enclosure was built from a different board than the one committed.
- Checks, run by `make check` or a sibling target:
  - each standoff hole is under its board hole; standoff tops stay within 3.5 mm of the hole centre on the board-facing sides;
  - a simple model of the board with its parts as boxes (heights above) does not touch the cover or base anywhere, with at least 1 mm to spare, and the antenna boxes have at least 2 mm;
  - each plug, the USB-C plug body and J2's wire path pass through their notches;
  - each zone light and D8 is under its opening, and each numeral is centred on its light;
  - the dial strip fits its recess and its window lines up with the cover's;
  - the cover can lift straight off: nothing of the cover is under the board, a plug, or the base lip;
  - no magnet pocket is within 20 mm of an antenna box;
  - clip holes match the clip's spacing and the clip bodies do not overlap each other or the box edge;
  - every part fits a 300 x 300 mm bed in its print orientation.
- The exported files are committed, like `fab/`, so the owner can print without installing anything.
- The carrier board README gains an "Enclosure" section: what to print, hardware list, assembly order, magnet polarity, and what was not verified.

## Left to the builder

- Exact shape of standoffs, lip, magnet pads, window rails and vent slots, within the rules above.
- How the script reads the board (from `carrier-board.kicad_pcb`, or by sharing constants with `gen_board.py`).
- How images are rendered.
- Chamfers and fillets.

## Known limits

- Nothing has been printed. Insert holes, magnet pockets and the cover's fit usually need one test print; the README should say to print a small test piece first.
- Part heights are from datasheets and estimates, not parts in hand. The owner should measure the tallest part on the assembled board before printing the cover.
- The lights sit about 20 mm behind the window, so they read best from straight on.
- The two bottom standoffs put a brass insert and a steel screw about 8 mm (in plan) from each antenna overhang, roughly 10 mm below it. The bare board already had screws there. The anti-goal on metal near the antennas covers the magnets, not these four fixings. If radio range disappoints, the first thing to try is nylon M3 screws in the two bottom corners; the README says so.
- The power light shows through an open 3 mm hole.
- Printed plastic has no flame rating. The board carries 5 V and switches 24 V AC from a class 2 transformer.
- The clip's release latch is under the box. Reaching it with a screwdriver from below is untried.
- The clip screw heads stand about 1 mm above the floor under the board, about 4 mm below the solder joints. They touch nothing but plastic.

## As built

Status: built and checked by software (`make -C hardware/carrier-board enclosure`); nothing printed. Files are in `hardware/carrier-board/enclosure/out/`, images in `hardware/carrier-board/renders/enclosure-*.png`, assembly in the carrier board README's "Enclosure" section.

**Outside size** of the closed box: 190.8 x 121.8 x 37.6 mm (base 9.0 mm high, cover 34.6 mm, the two overlap by 6.0 mm: 9.0 + 34.6 - 37.6; base from z -10.6, cover from z -7.6 to z 27 in the board frame). Inside 186 x 117 mm as specified.

**Handedness.** The model is built in the board frame (x right, y down, z toward the viewer), which is left-handed. Every exported solid and every render is mirrored in y (y -> -y) so it is the real part; the print orientations are then rotations and translations only. `test_exports_are_the_real_part` loads the exported STEP files back and checks it: the DIN clip slot (hook end) is at larger Y than the round hole, the power input notch is at smaller X and the zone notches at larger Y, and the strip's lettering is the y-mirrored lettering (and not the unmirrored one), with `1` left of `7` and `e` left of `t`.

**Exports.** Print orientations: base floor down; cover face down (turned 180 degrees about x); dial strip back down, lettering up, one body; pane flat. The plate is 1.8 mm thick (`strip_t`) in the 2.0 mm deep recess, so its front is 0.2 mm below the cover's face and the 0.4 mm lettering stands 0.2 mm proud of it; the lettering starts 1.8 mm from the bed, the filament change height (first black layer 10 at 0.2 mm layers). STL tolerance 0.02 mm, binary, each under 0.4 MB; `out/test-piece` and `out/test-piece-cover` (STEP and STL) are cut from the real base and cover for the first test print: the base's bottom-left corner (standoff, lip corner) and a bottom magnet pad with a stretch of lip; the cover's same two corners, which mate with them, and one end of the window with its rim, recess and a pane rail. `hashes.txt` records the hashes of `board-geometry.json`, `params.py`, `gen_enclosure.py`, the font, `board_model.py` and the export and render scripts, and a test fails if any has changed since the export.

**Changes from the design above, and why:**

- **The pane slides in along y, not x**, between rails at its two ends (the spec's "two rails ... slides in from one end against a stop at the other" is kept, with rails running along y). A rigid 126 mm pane cannot slide along x inside a 186 mm box whose window spans x 28 to 150 without going through a side wall, and tilting it in does not fit the 32 mm depth. It is laid flat on the inside of the face on the terminal-notch side of the rails (it cannot be lowered between them: their lips overhang its ends) and slides under the lips onto two stops; on the wall gravity holds it. It is held only at its two ends, so it can slide out when the cover is handled face down: fit it last.
- **Numerals and wordmark are 5.5 mm tall**, not about 3 mm. In Inter SemiBold at 3.5 mm several strokes (the `e` bar, the `1` flag, the `m` and `h` arches, the `a` bowl) are 0.3 to 0.45 mm. 5.5 mm is the smallest cap height at which every stroke is at least 0.5 mm. Inter's arch-to-stem joins stay about 0.3 mm wide at any reasonable size (the typeface thins them on purpose); the check is a morphological opening (no stroke thinner than 0.5 mm, no lost piece longer than 0.8 mm), not erosion connectivity. They print as slightly filled corners.
- **A 2.0 mm rim round the cover's window**, flush with the face (first 1.0 mm; widened to 2.0 mm after review as a sturdier anchor for the recess bridge; the strip's outline and lettering did not move). Printed face down, the plate behind the strip is a bridge over the strip's recess; without the rim the window's edge would hang free. The strip's window (126.5 x 16.5 mm) is therefore larger than the cover's (122 x 12 mm): the rim plus the 0.25 mm fit on each side. From the front there is a thin seam ring round the window. The strip's outline and lettering are laid out from the window grown by `strip_reach` (1.25 mm), so the look is unchanged by the rim.
- **The dial strip plate is 1.8 mm thick** in the 2.0 mm deep recess (`strip_t`), so a slightly sagging bridge or a glue layer does not push it proud; its front is 0.2 mm below the cover's face and the lettering stands 0.2 mm proud of the face (it was flush, 0.4 proud).
- **The dial strip is centred on the face left to right** (owner's decision): 142.7 x 42.1 mm, 24.05 mm from the box's left edge and 24.05 mm from its right edge. Its right, top and below-wordmark margins stay 4.0 mm; the left margin is derived (it grows by whatever centres the strip), so the strip extends left only and the window, numerals, wordmark and the strip's height and vertical position did not move. The window sits right of the strip's centre, because it follows the lights, and the strip stays nearer the top of the face than the bottom. The power light's hole is 7.4 mm from the recess's left end, so at least 2 mm of face remains (a test checks it).
- **Magnets meet at the lip top.** Pockets are exactly 3.0 mm deep (`magnet_recess` 0.0), and a seated pair has its faces 0.25 mm apart; the walls, not the pads, carry the cover. The cover's pads are short blocks with a 45 degree wedge to the wall, so the bottom wall's vent row stays free. Magnet pocket walls in the pads are 0.7 mm thick (test print).
- **Insert holes are 4.4 mm** (for the 5 mm OD x 4 mm insert), 7.0 mm deep.
- **Vents:** 25 slots, 2 x 10 mm, 4.5 mm pitch, centred on the bottom wall between the pads and the face.
- **The cover's rim bears on about 1.8 mm of the 2.4 mm wall**, the rest being the base rim's 0.6 mm chamfer.
- **Markings on the back** (`HOOK`, `UP`) are drawn as stroke block capitals, not in the font, 0.6 mm deep.
- **The clip counterbores** are 7.6 mm round and 7.6 x 9.1 mm at the slot.

**New in the README's text:** the carrier board README's DIN clip and "Distance to the rail" text now say which mounting applies: bare board (clips on the board, insulating sheet) or the enclosure (clips on the base, floor as insulation, no sheet).

**Not verified, as in "Known limits" above:** nothing printed; part heights are estimates; back-side component leads are not modelled (trim them flush); the lights read best from straight on.
